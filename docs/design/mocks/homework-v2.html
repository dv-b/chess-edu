Design pass · April 2026

Homework with submitted moves

Moving from a "done / skipped" self-assessment to a teachable unit of work: each homework is a sequence of blocks that students either solve (drag pieces, produce a line, submit) or study (step through an annotated game). Teachers review block-by-block; the student's homework status rolls up automatically. Mocks below cover the five scenarios in the brief and answer the open questions we discussed.

02 · Scenario

Homework detail — Solve block, two variants

Solve blocks are the new primary work surface. The student receives a position + prompt, plays the required sequence of moves on the board, and submits. Two variants below for how to signal "your turn to move" vs. "just watch".

Q1 answer — both signals layered. Variant A uses a prominent "Your move" chip and distinct block kind label ("SOLVE" in green). Variant B pushes further with a left-border accent and different card background tint. Recommendation: ship Variant A. The accent chip + kind label is enough, the background tint in B makes long homeworks feel like a mosaic. Keep the left-border accent from B as a subtle sub-treatment (already included in A below).

05 · Flow

Revisit a reviewed block — overwrite confirmation

Once a block is submitted, the student can still reopen it to practise the position. If they make a different move sequence, they get to choose: overwrite the submission (new attempt goes to the teacher for re-review), or keep practising in a sandbox.

Q7 answer — block-level + rollup. Each block gets its own review cycle; the homework's overall status in the student's list rolls up from its blocks. That means the teacher doesn't have to wait for all 6 blocks to be done before reviewing the first 3.

09 · Summary

Open questions · recommendations

Q1 — Solve vs. Study affordance. Layer multiple cues: green/blue kind label + left-border accent + "Your move" chip on solve blocks. Skip the full-background tint (Variant B).
Q3 — Homework-level self-mark. Replaced by auto-rollup. Keep "Mark as finished" as a tertiary micro-action that only appears when every block is terminal.
Q4 — Review granularity. Block-level, with a rollup surfaced on the class dashboard.
Q7 — Rollup. Most-urgent block wins: needs-work > awaiting-review > in-progress > ok > not-started.
Q9 — Solution after review. Unlock automatically once the teacher reviews. "Reveal" button on Needs-work states. Strict rule: no solution visible before review.
Deadline. Single pill, neutral → amber inside 24 h → red when overdue. Overdue doesn't lock the homework.
